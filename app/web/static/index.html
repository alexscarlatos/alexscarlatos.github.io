Dissertation

I'm a PhD candidate at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, working with Dr. Andrew Lan in the Machine Learning for Education (ML4Ed) lab. My research augments large language models (LLMs) with student-centered objectives to enable high quality and personalized educational AI systems. My recent work achieves this goal via methods for faithfully simulating student behavior using LLMs, which inform rewards or other signals for AI teachers.

I have published papers in NLP (ACL, EMNLP, NAACL) and education (AIED, LAK, EDM) conferences, but am also interested more broadly in machine learning, reinforcement learning, and musical applications of AI. I received my BS/MS in Computer Science from Stony Brook University, where I focused on musical interfaces and game development. Outside of research, my passions are writing and playing music, cinematography and photography, rock climbing, hiking, Chess, and video games.

Infinite 320

Infinite 320 is a gaming website/framework I created with frontend developer Jim Palmeri. Currently it supports two card games: Oh Shoot and No Thanks. Some features include a ranking/leveling system, AI players, player customization, and emotes.

Sonify

I developed this project for my undergraduate senior thesis and continued work on it for my Master's project. It's a tool for exploring the possibilities of sound design through spatial manipulation. Users move sound emitting and receiving objects through a room and draw sound barriers, hearing how the soundscape changes as they interact with the space.

CryptoCLASH

A multiplayer board game, centered around the vulnerabilities of digital currency. The game is meant to be a tool to educate on capitalism in a digital world, as well as examine behaviors of players under certain conditions. I built the game with two friends: Joshua Cehulic, who designed the game, and Marisa DePasquale, who created the assets.

Hybrid Synthesizer

A digital/analog hybrid, MIDI-controlled synthesizer, built with an Arduino microcontroller. Users can choose the wave generation function and modify envelope settings to achieve unique sounds. I wrote the code, and the electrical engineering was done by Collin Champagne.

Filmio

A tool developed for the purpose of syncing video to source audio for films. The code uses Praat to perform a cross-correlation on the video's audio and all external audio clips. Then based on the max value of the CC result and the start/end time of the matching segment, the optimal audio clip is selected and stitched onto the video. The audio volume is even optimally gained for an easier post-production experience.

Salaries of Iowa

My team and I analyzed the salary book for the state of Iowa employees over several years. We investigated a broad set of hypotheses, focusing on differences in salaries between genders, effects of a person's place of living on their income, effects of job mobility, salarly distributions between jobs, and the effects of inflation and world events on salaries.

ChordDFS

Source: Ion Stoica et al. Chord: A Scalable Peer-to-peer Lookup Service for Internet Applications. SIGCOMM, 2001.

My teammate and I developed a distributed file system on top of the Chord protocol, a technique for distributing and quickly retrieving resources in a non-centralized network. We implemented an original method for replicating data and rebalancing the network when a node fails.

Education Visualizer

A program that allows users to do statistical analysis, trimming, and visualization on education-related World Bank data. Multiple education variables and well-being variables are associated with nations across several years. The correlation between each pair of variables is calculated and displayed as a starting point for data exploration, and users can then drill down into the results and view graphs of any pair of variables. Users also have a variety of ways to filter the dataset, including by year, region, and k-means clustering.

Image Quilting

A program that, given a source texture, extends the texture or renders a target image using that texture. The algorithm originates in the 2001 paper "Image Quilting for Texture Synthesis and Transfer" by Alexei A. Efros and William T. Freeman.
